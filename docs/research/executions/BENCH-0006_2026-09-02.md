# BENCH-0006 — Provenance Ablation — Execution 2026-09-02

**Status:** `EXECUTED — narrow scope`
**Target claim:** CLM-0006 — provenance is necessary for trusted engineering evidence
**Corpus pinned at:** `4be7694`
**Tool:** `tools/bench0006/run_ablation.py` (deterministic, per ASSET-0006)

## 1. Design, and the trap avoided

CLM-0006's falsifier is *"target decisions remain equally reliable without provenance."*

The naive ablation — strip the provenance header, then ask provenance questions — is
tautological and proves nothing. The non-trivial question is **how much provenance survives
ablation because the document carries it in its own text**. Documents that state their own date,
version or supersession status remain auditable without external carriage; where that
self-carriage fails is exactly where carried provenance earns its keep.

| Condition | Evidence presented as |
|---|---|
| `P+` | content plus provenance header — commit, date, author, path |
| `P-` | the same content, header ablated |

Fifty evidence items (every committed version of every `.md`/`.json` in the corpus, excluding
tooling), of which **12 are non-current**. Three audit questions per item, ground truth from git:
attribution, dating, currency.

## 2. Result

| Audit question | P+ | P− | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| attribution — who produced this | 1.000 | 0.120 | 0.880 |
| dating — when | 1.000 | 0.480 | 0.520 |
| currency — is this superseded | 1.000 | 0.220 | 0.780 |
| **audit complete — all three** | **1.000** | **0.000** | **1.000** |

**Stale-evidence escape rate under ablation: 0.667.** Eight of the twelve superseded items carry
no in-content signal that a newer version exists. A reader holding one of those has no way, from
the document alone, to know it is stale.

**Weak cue:** 58% of superseded items at least self-date. That lets an attentive reader suspect
age; it does not let them confirm supersession, and it is not equivalent to carried provenance.

## 3. Reading

Content-only provenance recovery for full auditability is **zero**. Not one of fifty evidence
items survives ablation with all three audit attributes determinable — self-dating is common
(48%), self-attribution is rare (12%), and explicit supersession marking is rarer still (22%).

The falsifier is **not** met. CLM-0006 survives, within the scope stated below.

The interesting structure is in the gap between dating and currency. This corpus is
documentation-heavy and self-dates often, which is the most favourable case for content-embedded
provenance — and even here, knowing when a document was written does not tell a reader whether
it has since been replaced. Dating degrades gracefully under ablation; currency does not.

## 4. Scope and non-scope

**Scope.** Determinability of three audit attributes, measured by mechanical proxies, over one
APF repository corpus pinned at a single commit.

**Non-scope, and these are material.**

1. This measures **determinability, not decision reliability**. CLM-0006 asserts evidence is
   "materially less trustworthy for reuse"; undeterminable provenance is a necessary condition
   for that, not a demonstration of it. No decision was made and no reuse error was measured.
2. The claim's own benchmark direction calls for **expert agreement and reuse error**. Neither
   was collected. This run is the mechanical floor beneath that experiment, not a substitute.
3. Proxies are lexical. "Self-attribution" means the author string appears in the text; a
   document could attribute itself in a form the proxy misses.
4. Same corpus-independence limitation as BENCH-0004: the corpus is APF's own documentation.
   Self-dating rates in a code or measurement corpus would likely differ.

An earlier version of this run scored currency by the presence of any status or version string,
which returned a 0.000 stale-escape rate. That proxy was invalid — a document *having* a
`**Status:**` field says nothing about whether the copy in hand was superseded — and was
corrected to require an explicit supersession marker before any result was recorded.

## 6. Relation to H11

The stale-evidence escape rate measured here (0.667) is the same metric v0.2's `T11` specifies.
It does **not** adjudicate H11. T11 concerns *promoted assets* being revised, deprecated or
blocked once their basis goes stale — a lifecycle behaviour of the asset system. This run
measures whether a reader can *detect* staleness in raw evidence. Detection is a precondition
for invalidation, not the same measurement, and the asset ledger holds no accepted assets to run
T11 against.

The result does sharpen T11's design: if two-thirds of stale evidence is undetectable from
content, an invalidation mechanism cannot rely on documents announcing their own obsolescence.

## 7. Next

To attack CLM-0006 properly, run the expert arm the claim asks for: matched decisions made from
`P+` and `P−` evidence, scored on reuse error and expert agreement. That requires human judges
and is not executable here. Until then CLM-0006 stands on determinability alone.
