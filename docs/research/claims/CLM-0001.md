# CLM-0001 — Work-centric abstraction is more general than agent-centric abstraction

**Status of this record:** completed at benchmark preparation time per DEC-0002 option C.
**Written before execution.** `score.py` has not been run at the time of this commit.

## Why this claim, now

CLM-0001 is the highest-consequence claim in the inventory. `CONSTITUTION.md` section 2 — the sentence that makes APF something other than an agent framework — rests entirely on it. It requires no engineers, no users, and no target-domain access, and it has never been tested. It was placed last in the execution order because retrieval and provenance looked more tractable.

## Predeclared falsifiers

Primary metric: **mean concept coverage per workflow**, where coverage is the fraction of a workflow's source-declared concepts that the schema binds without an escape into a runtime-specific concept.

**F1 — primary.** Schema B's mean coverage does not exceed schema A's.
→ CLM-0001 is **falsified on CORPUS-0002**. The Constitution's section 2 boundary would need to be restated as a preference rather than a generality claim.

**F2 — boundary leakage.** Schema B escapes the core on more than one third of concepts overall.
→ The work-centric core does not hold the workloads it claims to hold; the claim is **CONTRADICTED** regardless of how A scores, because "better than agent-centric" is not the claim — "without importing agent-runtime concepts" is.

**F3 — no discrimination.** Both schemas exceed 0.95 coverage.
→ The concept vocabulary is too coarse to separate them. **INCONCLUSIVE**, not supportive.

**F4 — the word "both".** Schema B's coverage on the agentic class is below schema A's by more than 0.10.
→ The claim that a work-centric core supports **both** agentic and non-agentic workloads fails. The claim would be rewritten as a non-agentic-workload claim, which materially weakens the Constitution's framing.

F4 is the falsifier this benchmark exists for. An overall average can hide a schema that wins on four classes and loses badly on the one the platform is most often asked about.

## Preregistered analysis constraints

1. `schemas.json` is fixed at this commit. No binding may be changed after `score.py` runs. A binding I would want to revisit after seeing results is recorded as a defect, not edited.
2. Concept extraction (`extract.py`, `concepts.json`) ran before the binding table was written, so the vocabulary is fixed by the sources rather than chosen to suit a schema. It produced no scores.
3. Coverage is unweighted per workflow. Concept frequency across the corpus is not used as a weight, because corpus composition is a selection artifact.
4. A result not covered by F1 to F4 is recorded as a defect in this record.

## The limitation that cannot be engineered away

The binding table is authored by the same person who wants to know the answer. Every mitigation here is partial: the concepts come from third-party sources, the schemas come from their own sources, the table is committed before scoring, and the same criterion is applied in both directions — three concepts (`CONCURRENCY_CONTROL`, `TIMEOUT`, `RETRY_POLICY`) escape both cores, and four (`EVENT_SIGNAL`, `PROMPT_CONTENT`, `STATE_PERSISTENCE`, `STATE_SCHEMA`) escape B while binding in A.

None of that makes the author neutral. **This benchmark cannot reach the objectivity of BENCH-0004 Round 3**, where ground truth was written by strangers years earlier. A positive result here is weaker evidence than a positive result there, and should be read as "the boundary survived a structured attempt to break it", not as measurement.

The preferred evidence — an independent second annotator — is the fix, and it is not available in this session.
